HCAP • Creve Coeur Lake, Missouri

Managing invasive carp for the long term.

One huge removal helped in 2018, but flooding allowed carp to return. Our plan focuses on improving the levee against typical floods, monitoring the lake, and using smaller repeatable removals.

The problem

Why are these carp invasive?

Invasive carp are not originally from the Creve Coeur Lake ecosystem. They gather in large numbers, eat plankton needed by native fish, and disturb the lake’s food web. Silver carp can also jump when boats and other sounds startle them, creating a safety problem.

Creve Coeur Lake is a former bend, or oxbow, of the Missouri River. During floods, water can connect the lake to nearby waterways and give invasive carp a path back into the lake.

Past removal

The 2018 effort worked—but was difficult to repeat.

01

Concentrate

Agencies used boats, nets, sound, and other equipment to move fish into a smaller area.

02

Harvest

MDC, USFWS, USGS, St. Louis County Parks, and volunteers removed about 119 tons.

03

Return

The Missouri River flooded in 2019. Carp entered again, showing that removal alone is not permanent.

Main lesson: The plan must be affordable and repeatable, especially after floods.

Our proposed solution

A repeating management cycle—not a one-and-out event.

1

Improve the levee

The Howard Bend Levee District would lead the local levee work. It could hire a qualified engineering and construction company, while the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers reviews standards and permits.

→
2

Monitor

Survey the lake so crews know when and where another removal is needed.

→
3

Gather and remove

Use sound, boats, and corral-style nets with trained commercial fishers.

→
4

Transport and process

Ask Missouri Coast Fisheries of Farley, Missouri, to bring its mobile processing system or arrange pickup. The company turns invasive carp into bait, fertilizer, pet treats, and other products. This partnership would have to be confirmed before removal begins.

→
5

Repeat

Schedule smaller removals whenever monitoring shows the population is rising.

Why it could work

It uses methods already being tested

Recent work has tested underwater sound, semi-autonomous kayaks, and corral nets. Smaller operations should be easier to repeat than the 2018 effort.

Important limits

Flooding, processing, and experimental pellets

An improved levee could reduce entry during typical floods, but no levee can guarantee protection from every extreme flood. Missouri Coast Fisheries is a realistic processing partner, but pickup, price, permits, and capacity still must be confirmed. USGS is researching carp-targeting pellets, but they are experimental lethal controls—not approved sterilization pellets—so they are not part of our current plan.

Precedent

What Lake Winona taught us

1973: Officials treated the lake and installed an electric barrier and screens.

1974: Carp returned after the barrier failed and strong currents weakened a screen.

Later: Traps and another barrier helped at first, but debris clogged them.

Lesson: Connected waterways, currents, and maintenance can defeat a barrier. Creve Coeur needs monitoring and a backup plan.
